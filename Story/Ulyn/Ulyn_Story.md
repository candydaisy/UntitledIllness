# Ulyn — Busy

*The same semester, from the couch at his cousin's place, the student council room, and the seat next to Kay.*

Ulyn can't see inside Kay's head. Everything he thinks about Kay is a guess. Some guesses are right and some aren't.

## How to read this

The format is the same as `Story/Kay/Kay_Story.md`:
- Narration is present tense. Ulyn's thoughts are in *italics*. Text messages are `in code`.
- **◆ Choice** marks a decision, with its type, how it changes his Busy and Unsaid, and any flag it sets or needs. **◆ Call** is a decision made on the phone. **★** marks the **canon** choice (see `Story/Canon_Timeline.md`).
- **▸** marks what happens after each option. **◇** marks where the branches meet again. **[If …]** marks a passage that depends on a flag or on his meters.
- **📞** marks a phone call, **✂** a thought that gets cut off, **◌** a quiet line, and **⟨…⟩** a detail the player can click to notice.

### How Ulyn's route plays

Ulyn copes by staying busy. He has two hidden meters instead of Kay's Emotional Weight. The player never sees the numbers.

- **Busy** is how full he keeps his life. Taking things on raises it, and dropping them lowers it.
- **Unsaid** is everything he hasn't told anyone. It piles up while he avoids things and only goes down when he's honest. It decides the ending.
- **Choice types:** `neutral`, `honest` (about someone else), `helpful`, and `open` (honest about himself).
- **Locks:** `open` choices lock while Busy is above 80. Honest and helpful choices never lock: he can always help someone else.
- **📞 Calls** ring for 6 seconds instead of showing choice buttons. Letting one ring out declines it. Some calls only have one button, so the player has to decline his mom themselves.
- **✂ Cut-off thoughts:** at Busy 60 or more, a thought about home breaks off mid-sentence and turns into a task.
- **◌ Quiet lines** type slowly and can't be skipped or auto-advanced. While he's busy, every other line types faster.
- **⟨Noticing⟩:** some details about other people can be clicked. A choice marked *needs* `Flag` only appears if he noticed it.

**Start:** Busy 60, Unsaid 50. **Endings:** Unsaid below 35 → *Home* · below 65 → *Most Nights* · otherwise → *Busy*.

### People only Ulyn's route introduces

- **Tan**: Ulyn's cousin, 21, a university student. His apartment is ten minutes from school. Ulyn has slept on his couch most nights since March.
- **Mom**: Ulyn's mom. She works at a bank. Since Ulyn's dad moved out, she lives alone in a house built for three.
- **Dad**: moved across the city. Calls on Sundays. Ulyn doesn't pick up.
- **Pim**: student council president. Organized to the point of being frightening.

## Chapter 2 — Group 3

History. Group 3 stands at the front of the room. There's a gap between each of them, about one person wide.

Kay does the introduction in his presentation voice, steady and friendly, smiling with exactly the right amount of teeth. To everyone else, Ulyn guesses, he looks perfectly fine. Ulyn watches his eyes. They keep sliding across the room, landing on nothing.

Ulyn does his bit. "I made the slides, so if they look good, you're welcome." People laugh. It's easy. Being funny is the easiest thing Ulyn knows how to do.

Then it's Bank's turn.

"So Obama was... he was born in..."

Bank looks at his phone. Scrolls. Scrolls again. The silence gets long enough to hear the air conditioner.

*He never read it. He never even opened it.*

Ulyn looks at Kay. Kay's face doesn't move, but his hands do. ⟨One thumb presses into the other palm, hard.⟩ *(notice → `NoticedKayThumb`)*

*Let him blank, Kay. Just once. Let it be someone else's problem.*

"—Hawaii, 1961. Sorry, I'll jump in, we split this part."

*Of course.*

Kay knows every date. Of course he does. He wrote every line. Fern points at a slide she has clearly never seen before, and Kay steps in again, so smoothly it looks planned.

"Very well organized, group 3," the teacher says. "Good teamwork, everyone."

"Nice one, Kay! You saved us."

"Seriously, you're the best."

"Ha... it's nothing."

Ulyn doesn't say anything. He doesn't look at Bank or Fern. He looks at Kay, who is smiling at them with his whole face except his eyes.

"It would've been fair. You wrote his part. You wrote her part. And they get the same grade as you."

"Last semester it was the science poster. Before that, the English skit."

There's a lot of frustration in his chest. Some of it is about Bank and Fern. More of it is about Kay. Most of it, if Ulyn were being honest, which he isn't, is about something else.

## Chapter 3 — Lunch

"Hey, so... I can't eat with you today. My chemistry report's due tomorrow and I haven't started."

"Oh. That's fine! Go."

Kay says it to his tray. ⟨He hasn't touched his fried rice.⟩ *(notice → `NoticedKayTray`)*

*That's fine. Exclamation mark.*

"You sure? I can put it off and just do it tonight."

He means it. He would. He'd be up until four, and he'd do it gladly, if Kay asked.

Kay looks at him for a long moment. Ulyn has the strange feeling that a whole conversation is happening behind Kay's eyes, one he's not invited to.

"I'm good. Really. Don't worry about me."

*Don't worry about me.* Ulyn could make a list of every time Kay has said that, starting last April.

"...Okay. Save me a seat in class, then."

*I bet he's drowning them.*

Ulyn doesn't know why that thought makes his chest go tight. He writes *overwatering* in the margin of his notebook and draws a box around it.

## Chapter 5 — The Garden

Tuesday.

He finished the chemistry report at three. He lies with his forehead flat against the lunch table.

"Finished the chemistry report at three. Don't look at me."

He feels Kay looking at him anyway. Neither of them says anything about three in the morning, or about how Kay probably knows exactly what it feels like.

---

In homeroom the teacher writes **COMMUNITY PROJECT** on the board.

"Kay! Group?" Bank is already dragging his chair over.

*Of course.* "He's with me. You two can come, I guess."

When they're arguing about topics, Ulyn looks out the window. The four raised beds behind the science building are full of weeds as tall as a kid. He walks past them every morning from Tan's place.

He remembers Mom's balcony. Before March it was full of herbs and a lime tree in a big blue pot. Now it's full of brown sticks.

*Nobody was home to water them.*

## Chapter 7 — Sports Day

The fourth Friday. Sports day.

Ulyn runs it all with a clipboard and a megaphone he doesn't really need. The relay starts on time. The tug-of-war rope arrives. Nobody faints. Pim hugs him. Teachers he's never talked to tell him "great job."

It feels like a warm room again. He tries to stay in it.

At the water station, Kay is filling paper cups with a clipboard under his arm. Ulyn didn't ask him to volunteer. *Of course he volunteered.* He looks grey, the way old newspaper is grey.

*He looks like he hasn't slept since the Obama presentation.*

Ulyn sees Elysia run up to the station with a cold drink, push it into Kay's hands, say something, and sprint away before her friends can catch up. Kay stands there holding it like it's made of glass.

At 2 p.m., Ulyn sees his mom in the stands.

She's in her work clothes and must have taken the afternoon off. She waves. Small. Unsure.

*Why is she here.*

## Chapter 9 — Where Are U

The sixth Thursday.

Kay isn't at the gate. Ulyn doesn't think about it. Kay sometimes goes straight to the garden.

Kay isn't in homeroom either.

*Kay is never late. Kay came to school with a fever in tenth grade and threw up in the bin by the stairs and then sat through math.*

`where are u. ur never late` (8:10)

Delivered. Not read.

8:30. 9:00. 9:30. Not read.

Ulyn sits through chemistry and doesn't hear a word. He checks his phone under the desk eleven times.

*He's sick. People get sick.*

*He'd answer if he was just sick. He'd send a thumbs up. He'd say "just a cold! don't worry!" with an exclamation mark.*

---

At lunch, before he can sit down, Elysia is standing at the end of his table.

She doesn't have her friends with her. She isn't smiling. Ulyn has never seen her not smiling. It's like seeing a teacher in a supermarket.

"Can I talk to you? Just for a second."

They stand by the drinks fridge.
