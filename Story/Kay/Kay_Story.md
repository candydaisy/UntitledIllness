# Kay — Overwatering

*One semester in Kay's life, told from his side.*

Chapter 1 (Monday) is already playable in `flow_graph.json`. This file picks up the next morning and runs to the last day of the semester, about ten weeks later.

## How to read this

- Narration is present tense. Kay's thoughts are in *italics*. Text messages are `in code`.
- **◆ Choice** marks a decision. Each option lists its type, its weight change, and any flag it sets.
- **▸ A / ▸ B** are what happens after each option. **◇** marks where the branches meet again.
- **[If `Flag`]** marks a passage that only plays if that flag was set earlier. **[Otherwise]** is what plays if it wasn't.
- **[If weight above 60]** works the same way, using Emotional Weight.
- **★** marks the **canon** choice. See `Story/Canon_Timeline.md`. Day 1's canon choices are listed there.

## Chapter 2 — Tuesday

Kay wakes up at 6:10, before the alarm. He lies still and lets the ceiling get lighter.

**[If `OpenedUp`]**
The first thing he sees when he unlocks his phone is last night's chat.

`lunch tmr? just us. my treat`
`ok`

He reads it three times.

*I said it. I actually said it.*

He waits for the embarrassment to arrive. It does, a little. Something else comes with it, something quieter, and he doesn't have a name for it yet.

**[Otherwise]**
He checks the group chat out of habit. Nothing new. A sticker from Bank at midnight. A meme Fern forwarded from somewhere.

`it kind of stung tbh`

`but thank u for saying sorry 🫶`

**▸ B.**

Kay laughs out loud, alone in his room. It surprises him.

**▸ B.**
`boooo. ok!! tomorrow then 😤`

He doesn't have to stay late. He just remembers her friends watching yesterday, the one who whispered.

**◇**

The shower is still cold. He washes fast and tells himself it wakes him up better anyway.

---

Homeroom. The teacher writes **COMMUNITY PROJECT** on the board in big letters and underlines it twice.

"Groups of four. Find something in our school community that isn't working, and fix it. Not a poster. Not slides. *Fix* it. Last week of the semester, you present what you did."

The class groans as one.

Before Kay can turn around, Bank is already dragging his chair across the floor. Fern is right behind him.

"Kay! Group?"

Ulyn leans back in his seat. "He's with me. You two can come, I guess."

So it's group 3 again. Same four. Same careful gaps.

"Can we do the vending machine?" Bank says. "It eats coins. That's a community problem."

"Kay, you pick," Fern says. "You always pick good stuff."

Kay opens his mouth, but Ulyn is already pointing out the window, past the science building, at four raised wooden beds gone wild with weeds.

"The garden. The agriculture club died two years ago. Nobody's touched it since."

The teacher looks out the window, then back at them. "Approved. That's actually a good one, group 3."

"Ooh," Fern says. "Aesthetic. I can do before-and-after photos."

"Is gardening, like, physical?" Bank asks.

"Yes, Bank," Ulyn says. "It's physical."

Fern turns to Kay with her pen already uncapped. "So you'll do the report and the plan, right? You're so good at that."

Bank nods like it's already decided.

**[If `Overheard`]**
*"He doesn't mind, though. He offered."* Yesterday plays back in his head, word for word.

**◆ Choice**
- **A. ★** "Sure. I'll handle the report." (neutral, +8)
- **B.** "Let's split it. Everyone writes up the part they work on." (honest, −5, sets `SplitWork`)

**▸ A.**
"Yay," Fern says. "Best leader."

Beside him, Ulyn's pen stops moving. He doesn't say anything. Somehow that's worse.

**▸ B.**
There's a small pause. Kay braces for it.

"Oh," Bank says. "Yeah, sure. That's fair."

"Okay!" Fern writes it down. "Then I'll do the photos and the design part."

*That's it?* He had three arguments ready. He didn't need any of them.

Under the desk, Ulyn taps Kay's shoe with his own. Just once.

**◇**

Lunch.

**[If `OpenedUp`]**
Ulyn buys two plates of pad kra pao with crispy pork and a fried egg on each, and slides one across the table without a word.

Kay waits for the questions. *So what was "a lot"? What happened? Are you okay?*

"...You're not going to ask?" Kay says finally.

"Thanks."

"I mean, you *can*. Whenever. I'm just not going to make you present it."

"Was that a joke about the presentation?"

"A little bit."

It's the first lunch in a long time where Kay doesn't have to be anything.

**[If `UlynStayed`]**

"Worth it," he says.

"I'm sorry—"

"Say sorry one more time and I'm telling everyone you asked me to stay because you missed me."

Kay looks at his rice. "...I kind of did."

Ulyn goes completely quiet. Then he grins, way too wide, and doesn't stop for the rest of lunch.

**[Otherwise]**

"Finished the chemistry report at three," he says into the table. "Don't look at me."

Mom gets home in the middle of it. She stops in the bathroom doorway, still in her hospital shoes.

"What are you doing with the kettle?"

**◆ Choice**

**▸ A.**

**▸ B.**

**◇**

In bed, Kay stares at the ceiling.

**[If `SplitWork`]**

**[Otherwise]**

## Chapter 3 — Every Day

The garden behind the science building has four raised beds, weeds up to the knee, a hose with three holes in it, and a tap that screams when you turn it.

The group agreed on 8 a.m. Saturday. Kay gets there at 7:40.

Ulyn shows up at 8:20 with a bag of bread and no apology. Fern arrives at nine, takes before-photos for twenty minutes, and leaves for tutoring. Bank sends `my mom needs me today 😭🙏` at 9:15.

**[If `SplitWork`]**
Fern actually stays an extra hour. She pulls weeds and complains about her nails the whole time, but she pulls them. Bank turns up at ten, red-faced, holding four cold drinks like an apology.

*Huh*, Kay thinks.

**◇**

Ms. Noi, the home economics teacher, has agreed to be their advisor. She comes by at noon with a paper bag of seeds and a trowel she says is older than all of them.

"Water spinach. Basil. Chili. Marigolds around the edges, to keep the bugs off." She hands Kay the bag. "Water spinach is forgiving. Basil isn't. Chili sulks if you love it too much."

"Like Kay," Ulyn says.

"I don't sulk."

"Once a day," Ms. Noi says. "In the morning. *Only* if the soil is dry. Push your finger in, like this. If it's damp, leave it alone."

Kay writes it down in his notebook, word for word.

---

For two weeks, Kay comes to the garden every morning before homeroom and every afternoon after the last bell.

He pushes his finger into the soil the way she showed him. It's always a little damp. He waters anyway. Doing nothing feels wrong. Doing nothing feels like not caring.

Ulyn comes when he can, which isn't often. Sports day is coming, and the student council has put him in charge of half of it. He's also tutoring on Saturdays, and he's been sleeping at his cousin's place again. Kay knows that last part the way he knows most things about Ulyn now: from the edges, without asking.

---

One afternoon in the second week, Elysia comes to the garden alone. Her friends have gone to the mall.

She crouches beside the chili bed to look at the seedlings, and then she goes very still.

"Oh. *Oh.* Kay. Come here. Don't move fast."

On the old log edging the bed, something bright yellow has spread across the bark. It looks like spilled egg yolk, branching into veins like a tiny map.

"That's a slime mold," she whispers. "*Physarum*, I think. It's not a mold, it's not a plant, it's not an animal, it doesn't even have a brain, and it can still solve a maze. Scientists put oat flakes down like the cities around Tokyo, and it grew the train lines—"

She stops in the middle of the sentence and glances over her shoulder at the path. Then she laughs, too bright.

"Sorry. That's weird. Nobody cares about slime."

**◆ Choice**
- **A. ★** "I care. Keep going." (honest, −5, sets `ElysiaBugs`)
- **B.** "We should probably head back before it gets dark." (neutral, +5)

**▸ A.**
She stares at him.

Then she keeps going, slowly at first and then fast again. For twenty minutes she tells him about pink slime molds, slime molds that crawl a few centimeters an hour, and the one she kept in her lunchbox in third grade that found its way out of a paper maze overnight.

She sounds different. It isn't the loud cafeteria Elysia or the polite-smile Elysia. *This must be what she sounds like when she isn't checking.*

He wants to be someone she never has to check around.

When she finally runs out of words, she's still crouched by the log with her chin on her knees, looking at the slime mold like it's a puppy.

"Should I be jealous?" Kay says.

"Of *what*?"

"*Physarum.* You've never looked at me like that."

"*K!!*" She shoves his shoulder so hard he nearly sits in the chili bed. Her ears have gone red again. "It solved a *maze*. Without a *brain*. What have you ever solved?"

"Bank's slides."

"That's not a maze. That's a hostage situation."

He laughs, really laughs, and she looks so pleased with herself that he decides not to mention her ears.

**▸ B.**
"Yeah! Totally."

She stands up and brushes off her knees. On the walk out she talks about her friends' mall trip, about a café, about a lot of things. She doesn't mention the slime mold again.

Kay has the feeling he missed something. He doesn't know what.

**◇**

"I'm always tired."

He looks up. She's asleep with her cheek on her textbook, and the phone is tilted so he can see half her face. She's making a very small noise, every few breaths. It sounds like a cat.

On Wednesday, between exams, Elysia drags him to the back table of the library.

"Phone." She holds out her hand. "It goes in my bag until we finish the chemistry."

"Yes, ma'am."

"*Stop* that." She's made flashcards. Forty of them, color-coded, with tiny slime molds drawn in the corners of the hard ones. She quizzes him. He gets the first eleven right.

"Ugh. You're *annoyingly* right."

"I made them *hard*."

He doesn't remember deciding to close his eyes.

---

When he opens them, his head is on her shoulder.

The clock above the desk says forty minutes have gone by. Elysia hasn't moved. She's holding the same flashcard she was holding before, and her phone is face down on the table, buzzing every so often. She's letting it buzz.

He sits up so fast the chair squeaks.

"Sorry. *Sorry.* Your arm—"

"My arm is dead," Elysia says calmly. "It's fine. I've accepted it." She shakes it out and winces. "Also, you drool."

"I do *not*."
